01 — Three routes

Where ARC sits in post.

[ 01 ]

The freelance editor

Technical editing without narrative criterion. No serious motion, or motion subcontracted to someone else. You direct the cut, you bring the references, and the piece ends up disconnected from the rest of your brand.

[ 02 ]

The in-house / agency route

Bundled into an expensive retainer, diluted across eight accounts. The brief passes through four hands before the first cut. Long calendars, deck-driven. Generic templates wearing your logo.

[ 03 ]

The studio route

Edit and motion in the same hands. Narrative criterion before the first cut. One voice across film, site and agent. Stage discipline applied to the frame — the piece arrives ready to be watched twice.

04 — Process

From footage to ship.

W1

Listen + Frame

What the piece has to say. Who watches it. Where it lives. We screen the footage together, agree the line of argument, lock the structure before a single clip is moved.

W2–3

Make

First cut. Motion graphics built in parallel. Sound design draft. We share a watchable version before it's polished — early enough to change direction if needed.

W4

Rehearsal

Refined cut. Color, sound mix, motion polish. Two rounds of revisions, calibrated against the brief — not against opinion.

W5–6

Ship

Master files, social cutdowns, subtitle tracks, project files. Delivered in a clean folder structure your team can navigate without us.

∞

Versions

New cuts, additional languages, repurposed edits for next quarter. Same studio, no re-onboarding. The film keeps earning its keep.

06 — Honest answers

The questions we get most.

Do you shoot? +

No. ARC is a post-production studio — edit, motion, color, sound, finishing. If a project needs a shoot, you bring the footage, your team captures it, or we point you to a crew we trust and stay close to the brief so the edit doesn't fight the material.

Why €3,500 when there are editors at €800? +

The €800 editor cuts what you tell them to cut. ARC asks what the piece is supposed to do before the first cut, builds motion in the same room, and ships with sound and color finished. Different scope, different price. If €800 is the budget, an editor is the right call — we'll point you to two we respect.

What about stock footage and music? +

Sourcing and standard licensing for internal use is included on most projects. Extended licensing — paid media, broadcast, regional TV — is quoted separately because the rights window changes the fee. We'll tell you up front which bucket your use case falls in.

Who owns the project files? +

You do. Master files, source projects, exported subtitle tracks, motion comps — handed over on delivery. No lock-in. If you want to keep iterating elsewhere later, you can.

Can you work in Spanish, English and Portuguese? +

Yes. Subtitling, voiceover direction and motion versioning across the three. Other languages on request — we work with translators we've used for years.

What happens after delivery? +

The film is yours. New cuts, language versions, anniversary edits — same studio, no re-onboarding. Quoted per request, no retainer required.
